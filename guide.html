Binocular Motion Lab guide

Binocular motion simulator by Honghao Zhao. Two rotational degrees of freedom per eye, with a fixed head.

Choose an input

  • Live camera: select a front/rear camera, start it and show one index fingertip. The front preview and overlay are mirrored; the rear preview is natural. Detection uses original image coordinates. RGB depth is assumed, not measured.
  • Dataset replay: compare controllers against bundled held-out GazeBaseVR recordings. This is recorded human behavior, not a live patient measurement.
  • Synthetic target: explore horizontal, vertical, depth, circular or step movement, or set a fixed point.

Touch controls

On phones, the eyes and hand-camera preview stay together on the main screen. Landscape places them side by side. Open Tools for Setup, the optional 3D Scene, Results and Help. Start or stop the camera and pause eye motion using the bottom bar. For replay or synthetic input, the 3D target replaces the hand preview. Drag the main 3D canvas to rotate the scene; pinch or use + / − to zoom. Reset view restores the starting camera. Scroll using the area outside the main 3D canvas. Wide result tables scroll horizontally.

Controllers and exports

Ideal geometric gaze points toward the target. The expanded-data GRU is the default; the long-training GRU remains as a comparison. Model selection and evidence. Human replay is available only for recorded data. Pause freezes eye motion without stopping the camera. Export CSV saves up to 120 seconds of the current run; it does not save video.

Calibration

The default geometry uses assumed camera intrinsics and target distance. Optional camera/screen calibration requires measured screen dimensions, a fixed camera, camera binding, matching resolution and supported fullscreen. Do not treat a handheld or rotated phone as a fixed calibrated setup. Browsers without element fullscreen can still use the ordinary simulator, but cannot activate the physical fullscreen calibration workflow.

Virtual patients

Open Generate patients, select a template and side, and create a numbered library. Use patient activates a fixed patient by ID. Save & export contains library backups and run downloads. See the patient guide and mathematical model. Patient parameters are reproducible research candidates; NIHSS scores are not assigned.

Research limits

Controller results compare motion against selected human recordings. They do not validate a physical robot, clinical gaze recognition, real fingertip depth or behavior outside the dataset. The gaze-tracking application is a separate package.

Data source

Adapted, filtered/resampled excerpts of GazeBaseVR by Dillon Lohr, Samantha Aziz, Lee Friedman and Oleg V. Komogortsev. Dataset · CC BY 4.0 · Paper. Attribution does not imply author endorsement.

Camera orientation

Front-camera video and its fingertip overlay use a mirror preview. Rear-camera video uses a natural preview. The active track’s facing mode takes precedence over the menu selection when reported. In the uncalibrated camera mode, the eye display follows the preview direction for either camera. This display transform does not change physical target coordinates or exported angles. Explicitly calibrated display geometry remains unchanged. Camera geometry, the optional 3D scene and research charts are collapsed by default.
