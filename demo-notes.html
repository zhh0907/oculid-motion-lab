Continuous response

C = 45c = 5.4°    k = 2    τ = 0.12 s
u = r + k(r − q)
If d·u > 0: u_out = d·C·tanh(d·u/C)
Otherwise: u_out = u
τ·dq/dt = u_out − q

r is the normal reference angle, q the patient angle, and d the fixed impaired direction. Capacity and direction remain fixed throughout a run. The drive function and its first derivative are continuous at zero. With a fixed reference, the response has a stable equilibrium. Drive saturates smoothly; eye angles are not clipped at a fixed threshold.

These checks establish internal consistency and numerical behaviour, not human physiological validity. The affected axis has response dynamics in both directions. Vertical and unaffected channels retain the normal output exactly.

A common tracking task

The target moves between ±0.36 m at a depth of 0.70 m (about ±27.2°), with 1.5-second holds. Motion uses quintic easing, 10a³ − 15a⁴ + 6a⁵, so velocity and acceleration reach zero at hold boundaries. This is a slow following task with no added saccade examination.

Camera input uses the same model for arbitrary valid 3D targets, with assumed RGB depth. Tracking loss freezes eye position and state. Case selection, restart and input changes reset the run. Repeating the same input and initial state reproduces the output. Face shape changes only the rendered head.

Clinical motivation and limits

Bronstein and colleagues’ clinical, eye-movement and MRI study supports distinguishing conjugate gaze disorders from adduction impairment. An MRI case study of an abducens nuclear lesion describes horizontal conjugate gaze impairment. These sources motivate directional patterns; they do not provide this demo’s capacity, timing, gain or tanh parameters.

The adduction template omits possible abducting nystagmus and separate convergence pathways. The abduction template does not reproduce every resting-position or associated sign of abducens palsy. Neither is a validated complete lesion model.

Official NIHSS instructions require assessment of examination findings. Pronounced limitation does not automatically imply a score of 2. This page provides no reflex-test evidence and leaves plausibility and scoring to clinicians.
