METHODS / 2026.10.29

Preserve gaze. Add a moving face.

Eye movement, eyelid appearance and image observations are separate layers. Oculid renders the existing gaze angles through animated eyelids. Blinking and face shape do not change the underlying gaze controller or patient parameters.

Target → Original GRU / geometric reference → Patient layer → Four eye angles
Eye angles → Eyelid motion and blinking → Connected 3D face and eyes
The same angles → Synchronized eye-only comparison

Male and female face shapes

Version 0.8 provides two authored shape presets. They differ in jaw width, chin contour, nose, cheeks and brow shape. Both derive from the same licensed base scan; the female preset is a geometric adaptation, not a second scanned individual. These presets describe appearance choices, not population measurements.

Switching face shape preserves the active patient, gaze state and trace history. Run JSON records the initial face shape and subsequent changes with simulation timestamps. Skin tone remains a separate choice with five presets.

Connected three-dimensional geometry

The head and animated eyelids share mesh-boundary vertices. The eye has an open-front sclera, recessed iris with a pupil opening, dark inner cavity and an independent convex cornea. Skin detail is procedural; no facial photograph is loaded. Rotate the view or choose Clay / Wireframe to inspect these structures.

The base asset is Lee Perry-Smith’s Infinite, 3D Head Scan, based on Triplegangers’ work and distributed under CC BY 3.0. Source: Three.js r180. Changes include alignment, subdivision, socket cuts, connected animated lids and shape deformation. Detailed geometry notes (Chinese) ↗

Compatibility evidence

3,600 upstream reference samples retain exactly equal four-angle outputs.

Automated checks cover fixed patients, motion invariance, eyelid closure, connected geometry, face switching, exports, replay, camera-input boundaries and mobile layouts. Camera checks inject known targets; they do not measure physical camera accuracy. See the validation record.

Fixed patients and live traces

Choose a movement pattern and side, then generate numbered patients with fixed parameters. Sampling is balanced across engineering capacity strata. The library can be recalled by ID or transferred by JSON. No NIHSS scores are assigned.

Time plots and 2D traces show simulated eye angles in degrees. Solid lines are current gaze; dashed lines are the normal-model reference for the same input. Position rails show a fixed frontal geometric projection of the pupil centre. These are simulation values, not eye positions measured from images.

Eyelid behaviour and target range

Engineering presets provide irregular repeatable blinks, faster closure than opening, gaze-following lids and simplified corner tissue. The target range can be adjusted independently from the motion model. Wide-angle GRU behaviour remains exploratory. These changes do not alter fingertip mapping or patient parameters.

What remains unvalidated

Eyelid timing, shape, tissue deformation and appearance parameters are engineering approximations, not fits to human eyelid measurements. The renderer does not provide complete tissue mechanics or clinical optical ray tracing. Pupil visibility is a frontal geometry estimate and does not describe arbitrary rotated views or tracking confidence.

Training a tracker or scoring model still requires clinician review and evaluation on independent real data. Full closure removes visible pupil information even though simulated gaze angles remain available.
